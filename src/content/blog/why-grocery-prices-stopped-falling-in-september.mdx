For most of the year, the trend at the grocery store pointed in one direction. Prices on staples such as eggs, dairy, and fresh produce eased month after month, giving households some room after a long stretch of increases.

September broke that pattern. Prices did not jump, but they stopped falling. For shoppers, that means the relief they had come to expect at the checkout is, for now, on pause.

## What the numbers show

The food-at-home category flattened after several months of small declines. The change was not uniform. Some items, including produce and poultry, held steady, while packaged goods continued to edge lower.

A flat month on its own is not a reversal. It does, however, suggest that the easiest gains from lower input costs may already be behind us.

## Three reasons the decline stalled

* **Transport costs.** Diesel prices rose over the late summer, adding cost to every truckload that reaches a store.
* **Weather.** A hot, dry stretch in key growing regions reduced yields for several crops.
* **Labor.** Wages across food processing and retail continue to rise, and those costs are slow to come down.

> “A pause is not a reversal. But it does tell you the easy part of the decline is over.”

## What it means for your budget

For a typical household, a flat month changes little on its own. The bigger question is direction. If costs for transport and labor stay elevated, shoppers should expect prices to hold near current levels rather than continue falling.

Store brands, seasonal produce, and planning around weekly promotions remain the most reliable ways to keep a grocery bill in check while prices settle.

## What to watch next

The next monthly price report will show whether September was a pause or the start of a new trend. Fuel prices and the fall harvest are the two signals most likely to move the numbers either way.
